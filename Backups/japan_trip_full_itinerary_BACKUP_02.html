01
Osaka
Apr 18–21
3 NIGHTS
Trip days 1–3
Japan's anti-Tokyo: louder, funnier, cheaper, more honest about its obsession with food. Osaka calls itself "the Nation's Kitchen" — a title it's held since the Edo period when it was the country's rice-trading hub. The energy here is street-level: neon-drenched food alleys, comedians busking in Namba, takoyaki stands with queues around the block. It's chaotic in the best way, and a perfect intro to Japan.

02
Kyoto
Apr 21–25
4 NIGHTS
Trip days 4–7
A thousand years of imperial history in a grid-pattern city framed by forested mountains on three sides. Kyoto is temples, tea, geisha, bamboo, and an almost absurd density of cultural heritage — 20% of Japan's national treasures are here, in a city with just 1% of its population. But it's also Nintendo's headquarters, a strong coffee scene, and backstreet izakayas where the food is extraordinary.

03
Onomichi
Apr 25–28
3 NIGHTS
Trip days 8–10 · Cumulative cycling: 50 km
A port town built on slopes so steep that cars can't reach most of its temples. Onomichi is all narrow alleys, stray cats sunbathing on stone walls, and hillside views over the Seto Inland Sea. It has a literary history — the setting of Ozu's 1953 film Tokyo Story — and today young creatives have moved in, turning abandoned hillside houses into cafes and galleries. Also famous for its soy-sauce-and-pork-fat ramen.

04
Ikuchijima
Apr 28–30
2 NIGHTS
Trip days 11–12 · Cumulative cycling: 83 km
Japan's "Lemon Island" — a sun-drenched Seto Inland Sea island that produces more lemons than anywhere else in the country. The air smells like citrus, the light has a Mediterranean quality, and the pace is gloriously slow. The main town of Setoda has an old-fashioned shopping street (shotengai) that's come back to life with specialty coffee, gelato shops, and a ryokan by the founder of Aman resorts.

05
Imabari
Apr 30–May 2
2 NIGHTS
Trip days 13–14 · Cumulative cycling: 125 km · Shimanami Kaido complete!
A working shipbuilding and textile city on the Shikoku side of the Shimanami Kaido. Not a tourist town — that's part of the charm. Imabari's claim to fame is towels: it produces 60% of all towels made in Japan, an industry spanning 120+ years and nearly 200 factories. The towels are genuinely excellent (they must absorb water within 5 seconds to earn the Imabari brand), and they make a great souvenir.

Imabari's towel brand was saved from extinction by designer Kashiwa Sato in 2006. Production had dropped 80% due to cheap imports. The rescue strategy? Compete on perfection, not price. The iconic red-white-blue logo was born, and "Imabari Towel" became a national brand.

Cycle to Uchiko
Imabari → Uchiko via inland Ehime
~80 km Full day (6–7h) Hilly inland terrain
Head south from Imabari into the Ehime highlands. The route climbs through river valleys and forested hills before dropping into the Oda River basin. A demanding day with significant elevation gain — the payoff is arriving in Uchiko's historic district from the quiet north, with virtually no tourist traffic on the road.
06
Uchiko
May 2–4
2 NIGHTS
Trip days 15–16 · Cumulative cycling: 205 km
A pristine Edo/Meiji-era merchant town that made its fortune from wood wax and handmade paper. The Yokaichi Old Town district is one of the best-preserved historical streetscapes in Japan — white and yellow plaster warehouses, dark timber frames, stone-paved lanes. It feels like stepping into the 1890s, except there's a world-class kabuki theater around the corner.

07
Ozu
May 4–6
2 NIGHTS
Trip days 17–18 · Cumulative cycling: 217 km
Known as the "Little Kyoto of Iyo," Ozu is a castle town on the Hijikawa River that prospered through sericulture, lacquerware, and traditional papermaking. The old town has paved alleys lined with Edo and Meiji buildings — it's impossibly quiet and photogenic. Recently, a NIPPONIA heritage hotel project has turned vacant historic buildings into boutique stays, breathing new life into the town.

Cycle to Matsuyama
Ozu → Matsuyama via Hijikawa River valley
~55–60 km 4–5 hours Mixed terrain, some hills
Follow the Hijikawa River downstream from Ozu, then cut north toward Matsuyama through gentle farmland and low hills. The terrain is mixed — flat river valley sections alternate with hillier stretches as you approach the city. A satisfying final cycling day before the long train north to Tokyo.
08
Matsuyama
May 6–8
2 NIGHTS
Trip days 19–20 · Cumulative cycling: 275 km
Shikoku's largest city and the last stop before Tokyo. Matsuyama wraps around a hilltop castle and extends out to Dogo Onsen — the oldest hot spring in Japan, with a 3,000-year history. The bathhouse directly inspired the one in Miyazaki's Spirited Away. The city also has a literary connection: Natsume Soseki set his novel Botchan here, and there's a replica steam train named after it.

09
Tokyo
May 8–13
5 NIGHTS
Trip days 21–25
The biggest possible gear-shift: from cycling through Shikoku rice paddies to the largest metropolitan area on earth. Every neighbourhood is its own world — Shimokitazawa for vintage shops, Yanaka for old-Tokyo temple streets, Daikanyama for design bookshops, Roppongi for museums. For a design-minded person, this is the motherlode: Tadao Ando buildings, Issey Miyake's museum, teamLab's immersive worlds, and more independent galleries than you could visit in a month.
